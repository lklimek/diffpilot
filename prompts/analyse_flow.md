REVIEW SCOPE: production changes only. Do not plan, title, walk through, annotate or assess test code, fixtures, mocks, benches or test helpers. Never create a flow whose subject is tests. Test code may be read only as evidence for a production step and mentioned in one clause there.
Lines marked "context only" in the hunk list (Rust comments, plain private `use` lines, lines whose only change is whitespace, blocks moved with identical text into another file) are context: they need no explanation, step or flow. Exception: when such a line changes behaviour (an import that changes which trait method, constant or pattern a name resolves to; a doc comment read by a derive macro such as clap or displaydoc, or a doctest; a moved block that means something else in its new place), cover it in a flow like any other change. Every other changed line of a listed hunk needs a flow or `unassigned`. A comment contradicting code may be cited on the production-code step.

Task: analyse flow {{flow_id}} for the reviewer. Set `flow_id` to "{{flow_id}}".
- `introduction`: `problem_before` (the local trigger and the defect or gap before this change), `change` (the intended correction), `example_before_after` (a short concrete example).
- `steps` in reading order: start at the trigger (the entry point) and follow control flow; one line range of one file per step. Every production changed line of the flow's hunks lies inside a step, except context-only lines. Removed lines lie at their deletion marker inside a head step, or in a `base` step of kind `deleted_site` when no head line is left. Do not create steps made only of unchanged context: describe callers and connecting code in `called_by`, `next`, evidence and `impact_refs`.
- Step size: a step is what the reviewer reads at once, usually the change of one function or one decision. Put nearby hunks of one function into one step. Never split one statement, call expression or its arguments across steps; include the complete statement around a change. Never make a step of an import, a visibility change or a signature line alone: include it in the step that needs it. Repeated mechanical edits are explained once and listed in `pattern_repetitions` (their lines still lie inside steps).
- `decides`, `why`: one or two sentences each. Write optional `called_by` / `next` only for information the edges do not carry, such as an external caller or a condition; it follows the step targets derived from the edges, so never restate step ids or reading order.
- `annotations` inside their step: for `type: behaviour`, `was` and `is` are nonempty before/after facts, without risk or verdicts; omit `text`. For `consequence`, `flag` and `note`, omit `was` and `is` and put the content only in nonempty `text`. Never copy behaviour into another annotation. A behaviour annotation and a flag may share lines.
- In the tagged file omit empty optional fields. Use the short file/hunk ids from `sr-check catalog out/flow.md`; do not repeat paths or the locations/kind of a planned hunk. Prose and code are raw text (use a heredoc for multiline values).
- Flags: raise a `flag` only for a concrete risk scenario you can state: who acts, the order of events, the outcome and its cost. If you cannot state them, it is not a flag; write a `note` if it still helps the reviewer. Look through the review lenses: callers not updated, error and boundary cases, concurrency, compatibility, missing tests. A risk you can settle by reading is not a flag: read, then state the fact. `level: red`: a likely defect or damage if the scenario is reachable; `level: yellow`: worth a look, plausible but weaker or of lower cost. `text` is the title: the risk class, starting "Potential " and ending with ":" (Polish: "Potencjalny", "Potencjalna" or "Potencjalne"), for example "Potential TOCTOU between preflight and submission:". `scenario`: 2 to 7 steps, one per line, unnumbered. `not_checked`: one line saying what you did not reproduce or run. Plain text, no Markdown or links. At most 5 flags per step, the strongest; a risk shared by several steps is flagged once, where it arises, and a `note` on the other steps refers to it. There is no minimum number; never invent one. The step's rating is separate from flag levels, but a step with a red flag is never rated low.
- Assessment concerns unresolved risk this CHANGE introduces, worsens, exposes or leaves relevant to its intent: what may break or remain wrong after merging. A severe pre-existing bug that this PR fixes belongs in `introduction.problem_before` and PR-context `problem`, never in the change's risk band. `origin: pre_existing` is only for a still-present problem, not the fixed one.
- Apply the `severity` skill's production impact, likelihood and PR-scope guidance to that unresolved risk, against the project profile. Output only this schema's bands (`low`, `medium`, `high`); never skill report fields, numeric scores or merge-class verdicts. Do not rate the size or benefit of the fix as its risk.
- Every step requires an `### assessment`, including low-risk and context steps. Rate what the CHANGE may break or leave wrong, with its band and one sentence reason. For low, write only `risk` and `reason`; omit the other assessment fields. Above low, write the full assessment with evidence basis and concrete checks not performed. The flow risk is the highest step band. A rating is not a defect claim: never invent a defect or action. For a full assessment with no blocking action or follow-up proposed, use `blocking: no_action_proposed` and omit `smallest_correction`; low needs only the bounded exposure in its one-sentence reason.
- Full `assessment` above low: `headline` = the unresolved production effect; `risk` = its band; `reason` = why (also for low); `basis_note` = what you did: `Read: <files, symbols, searches>` and, if you ran anything, `Ran: <commands>`; never claim that nothing was run; `not_checked` = concrete checks not performed, not missing agent capabilities. `blocking: blocks_this_pr` requires a concern to resolve before merge; `follow_up` requires a named action in `smallest_correction` (what to check/change and where), never a default for "nothing blocks". A non-low band needs a concrete `smallest_correction` or an evidenced `flag` on this step. No placeholder corrections such as "none given", "N/A" or "none"; omit `smallest_correction` when no correction is proposed.
- Omit `edges` when execution follows reading order; the server derives adjacent transitions. Otherwise write the complete edge list, with `label` for decision outcomes, loop back-edges and error exits.
- `impact_refs`: unchanged head code outside the diff that this change may affect (callers, implementers, readers of changed data), with the reason.
